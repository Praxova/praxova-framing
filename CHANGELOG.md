# Changelog

All notable changes to Praxova Framing are documented here. The project follows [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/) with the convention that pre-1.0 versions (0.x) are considered beta — APIs and data model may change between minor versions.

## [0.6.30] — 2026-05-17

### Fixed
- **L-joint corner tie-in: cap plates now lap correctly.** Cap plate geometry was identical to the lower top plate at each joint end — both shortened inboard at ABUTTING ends, both full at MAIN ends — which meant corners were butt joints with no mechanical tie. The cap is now the INVERSE of the lower top at each joint end: at a MAIN end the cap is shortened INBOARD by the paired wall's wall thickness (leaving room for the abutting wall's cap to land on top); at an ABUTTING end the cap is extended OUTBOARD past the wall edge by the paired wall's wall thickness, geometrically landing on top of the MAIN wall's lower top plate at the corner cell. This is the standard residential lap per IRC R602.3.2.
- **Asymmetric walls** (MAIN at one end, ABUTTING at the other — common on L-shaped floor plans) now produce a cap plate the same length as the wall but offset, with the cap reaching past the ABUTTING end and pulled inboard at the MAIN end. FREE ends (no joint) reach the wall edge unchanged, preserving v0.5 behavior for joint-less walls.

### Added
- **Two pure helpers in `templates.py`**: `lower_plate_end_offset(role, paired_t)` and `cap_plate_end_offset(role, paired_t)`. Deliberately inverse sign conventions — lower-plate offset is positive INBOARD (the plate gives way), cap-plate offset is positive OUTBOARD (the cap laps onto the paired wall). Module comment explains the inversion explicitly so the geometry is reasonable about without spelunking the operator. FREE role returns 0 for both, preserving v0.5 behavior.
- **Dedicated cap stock-fit check** in the frame_wall operator. The cap can now exceed wall length (ABUTTING-at-both-ends extends each end by paired_t), and that can exceed the nominal's max stock — for example a 16' (192") 2x6 wall ABUTTING-both with 2x6 paired walls would need a 17' (203") cap that doesn't fit in 192" stock. The operator catches this via `select_stock_length_in` wrapped in try/except and reports a clear error pointing the user at the v0.7-splice deferral plus the immediate workaround (set `extending_wall='A'` or `'B'` explicitly on the affected joint to make the shorter wall MAIN, sidestepping the lap on the long wall). Lower plates keep using the v0.5 `plate_stock_in` sized for `length_in` since they only ever shrink at joint ends.
- **Smoke test step `[28]`** queries `list_objects` after step `[27]`'s `frame_building` and asserts the lap geometry on both walls of the existing L corner: `wall_a` (ABUTTING at END) cap extends 3.5" past its wall edge to lap onto `wall_b`'s lower top plate; `wall_b` (MAIN at START) cap is pulled 3.5" inboard at its joint end. The `wall_a` cap at 99.5" is the regression sentinel — if v0.6.0's butt-joint cap geometry comes back, the cap drops to 92.5" and the step fails with a clear "v0.6.0 butt-joint geometry is back" message.

### Changed
- **Smoke test `addon_version` pin** "0.6.25" → "0.6.30". Method count stays at 18. Smoke test grows 30 → 31 steps; renumbered the three error-path steps to `[29]`/`[30]`/`[31]`.
- **README** Status line "Beta (v0.6.25)" → "Beta (v0.6.30)". Joints section gains a cap-plate-lap paragraph documenting the new geometry + the stock ceiling for ABUTTING-at-both walls + the v0.7 splice deferral.

### Known limitations
- Walls that are ABUTTING-at-both-ends with length close to maximum stock will hit a stock ceiling: a 16' 2x6 wall's cap would need to be 17' (203") to lap, exceeding 16' max stock. Such corners now error with a clear message pointing to v0.7 splice support. Workaround: set `extending_wall='A'` or `'B'` explicitly to make the shorter wall MAIN, which avoids the lap on the long wall.

### Bridge
- Method count: 18 (unchanged)
- `mcp_server` pkg version: 0.6.25 (unchanged — no schema or behavior change in the bridge surface; this is a pure geometry fix on the operator side)

## [0.6.25] — 2026-05-17

### Fixed
- **`extending_wall` now actually overrides at frame time.** Explicit `"A"` / `"B"` on a `PraxovaJoint` were stored but ignored by the framing operator — the interior corner-filler stud always landed on `wall_a` regardless of the field's value. Cause: `compute_wall_joint_context` hardcoded `role='MAIN'` for the `wall_a` slot and `role='ABUTTING'` for `wall_b`, bypassing `resolve_extending_wall`'s result entirely. Fix: `resolve_extending_wall` is now the single source of truth for role assignment. The wall whose slot matches the resolved extending letter becomes MAIN (gets full lower plates + corner filler + end stud at the corner); the other becomes ABUTTING (lower plates shortened by MAIN's wall thickness, end stud moved inboard).
- **`AUTO` `extending_wall` semantics**: `AUTO` now picks the **longer** wall as MAIN — matches the residential convention that longer walls form the outside and shorter walls butt in. Equal-length walls tie-break to `wall_a`. Previously the v0.6.0 docstring described "AUTO picks the shorter wall" but the implementation did neither — it always picked `wall_a` because of the role-hardcoding bug above. v0.6.25 makes both the docs and the implementation match the new semantics. `resolve_extending_wall` signature changed from `(joint, wall_a_length, wall_b_length)` to `(joint, wall_a, wall_b)` — takes Object refs and reads lengths itself, matching how `compute_wall_joint_context` and the new UI live-MAIN readout in the Building tab both want to call it.

### Added
- **Wall Length and Fill Length fields display in decimal feet.** New `wall_length_ft` (on both `PraxovaSceneProps` and `PraxovaWallProps`) and `fill_span_ft` (on `PraxovaSceneProps`) FloatProperty fields, backed by getter/setter pairs that proxy through the existing `_in` storage. The N-panel Wall Framing and Fill Edge-to-Edge sections bind these `_ft` fields instead of `_in`; the operators and bridge handlers keep reading inches directly. Existing `.blend` files need no migration — inch storage is preserved verbatim.
- **Live carpenter-format readout below each `_ft` field.** Small `= 12' 4.0"` label recomputes on every redraw so the carpenter sees exactly what their decimal-feet value translates to in feet-and-inches. `scale_y=0.7 + icon='INFO'` makes the readout visually subordinate to the editable field above. Display only this cycle — typing feet-inches strings ("12' 4\"") into the field for parsed input is out of scope.
- **Custom imperial-unit icon on the "Set Scene to Imperial" button.** Replaces the generic `SETTINGS` gear icon with a dedicated `imperial-unit.png` graphic loaded into a `bpy.utils.previews` collection. Standard Blender custom-icon plumbing (`register_icons` / `unregister_icons` / `get_icon_id`) in `__init__.py`; UI callers use `icon_value=get_icon_id("imperial_unit")` with a `SETTINGS` fallback when the collection isn't loaded (handles the module-reload edge case during development).
- **Live "Resolved MAIN" readout in the Building tab's joint detail panel.** Below the `extending_wall` dropdown, a `→ Resolved MAIN: <wall_name>` label calls `resolve_extending_wall` on every redraw and shows which wall is currently MAIN given the joint's current settings. Skipped gracefully when `wall_a` / `wall_b` haven't been picked yet. Critical context now that MAIN role follows `extending_wall` resolution rather than slot — without this readout the user has to mentally re-derive MAIN from a multi-field interaction.
- **Smoke test regression coverage for the `extending_wall` fix.** New step `[26]` calls `add_joint` with explicit `extending_wall="B"`; step `[27]` calls `frame_building` and asserts the corner filler lands on `wall_b` (not `wall_a`). Renumbered the three pre-existing error-path steps to `[28]`–`[30]`. Total: 30 steps (was 28). If the v0.6.0 bug regresses, step `[27]` fails with a clear "v0.6.0 bug is back" message.

### Changed
- **Joint detail panel labels softened.** `PraxovaJoint.wall_a.name` changed from `"Wall A (main)"` to `"Wall A"`; `wall_b.name` from `"Wall B (abutting)"` to `"Wall B"`. The parentheticals were accurate when `wall_a` was always MAIN; with the fix above they're no longer fixed roles. Property descriptions rewritten to point users at the live Resolved MAIN readout below the `extending_wall` dropdown rather than baking the old role assumption into the field tooltip.
- **`mcp_server` package version** 0.6.2 → 0.6.25. Description unchanged (joint-coordination capability already covered in v0.6.2 wording).

## [0.6.2] — 2026-05-12

### Added
- **Bridge `add_joint` + `remove_joint` handlers + FastMCP tools.** Joint lifecycle for the v0.6 multi-wall coordination model. `add_joint` writes directly to `level.praxova_level.joints` via `CollectionProperty.add` + field assignment — mirrors v0.5.1's `frame_wall` openings pattern of populating the collection directly rather than calling the UI op's two-step "append blank, fill in after." Required: `level_name` and a `joint` dict carrying `wall_a_name`, `wall_b_name`, `wall_a_end`, `wall_b_end` (all REQUIRED) plus optional `joint_type` (only `"L_CORNER"` valid in v0.6.x), `corner_style` (`"AUTO"` or `"THREE_STUD"`; defaults `"AUTO"`), `extending_wall` (`"AUTO"` / `"A"` / `"B"`; defaults `"AUTO"`). Wall name resolution rejects three failure shapes with `INVALID_PARAMS`: not in scene, not a Praxova wall, or parented under a different Level (cross-Level joints are out of scope). Returns `level_name`, `joint_index` (0-based), and a full filled `joint` dict.
- **`remove_joint` "undo handle" pattern.** Returns the full pre-removal joint spec — every wire field — so a caller that deleted the wrong joint can immediately call `add_joint` with the echoed `removed` dict to put it back, no scene-state caching client-side. `joint_index` validated against `[0, len(joints))` with the current count in the error message; `joints_active` clamped to the new bounds matching the UI op.
- **Bridge `frame_building` handler + FastMCP tool.** Wraps v0.6's `praxova.frame_building` orchestrator with the same three-step pre-flight the Building tab's live indicator runs: `_resolve_level`, gather walls under the Level (empty → `INVALID_PARAMS` naming the Level), `templates.validate_joints` (first failure surfaces verbatim with the `"Joint N: ..."` prefix). Returns aggregate fields (`n_walls_framed`, `n_joints_resolved`) plus a `walls_framed` list of per-wall summary dicts — each entry carries `wall_name`, `n_studs` (same semantics as `frame_wall`'s n_studs after opening filtering), `n_corner_fillers` (Object-name-based count of `CornerFiller_Joint<NN>` children per the v0.6.0 naming convention), and `joint_indices_touching` (PointerProperty identity comparison so wall renames don't orphan the index). Per-wall opening detail (jack lengths, header lengths, cripple counts) intentionally left to follow-up `frame_wall(wall_target=...)` queries — the orchestrator's response stays focused on joint coordination, which is the actual point of orchestration over N separate `frame_wall` calls.
- **Bridge `list_joints_in_level` handler + FastMCP tool.** Fills the gap v0.6.1's `list_objects` extension deliberately left — the LEVEL category there reported `joints_count` only because full joint serialization needed the joint wire format that v0.6.2's `add_joint` / `remove_joint` just standardized. Now that the format is fixed, this method returns the same per-joint dict shape `add_joint` produces, so a `list_joints_in_level` → mutate → `add_joint` round-trip works without translation. Per-joint entries include `joint_index`, `joint_type`, `wall_a_name` / `wall_b_name` (`None` when a UI-added joint hasn't had its walls picked yet — surfaced as `null` rather than crashing on `.name`), end + corner_style + extending_wall fields.
- **Shared bridge helpers in `server/handlers.py`** for the joint surface: `_resolve_level` (Object-name → Praxova Level lookup with the consistent `APP_OBJECT_NOT_FOUND` / `INVALID_PARAMS` error split), `_resolve_wall_in_level` (wall-in-Level validation with three failure shapes and field-name-aware error messages), `_parse_joint` (per-joint wire validation modeled on v0.5.1's `_parse_openings`), `_joint_to_wire` (PraxovaJoint → wire dict, handles `None` PointerProperty refs). Plus the four `_JOINT_*_CHOICES` tuples enforcing the wire-format enum contract without import-time coupling to `bpy`.
- **Smoke test 9-step extension** covering the full v0.6.2 joint flow. New steps `[17]`–`[25]` thread two walls (96"×96" 2x4 each, positioned with `location_m=[2.4384, 0, 0]` and `rotation_deg_z=90` for an L corner) through `add_building` → two `frame_wall(level=...)` → `add_joint` → `frame_building` → `list_joints_in_level` → rotation-refusal → `remove_joint` → rotation-success sequence. Step `[23]` is the v0.6.1 rotation+joints refusal that was specced into v0.6.1 but couldn't be tested then (no `add_joint`). Step `[25]` confirms removing the joint clears the refusal. Renumbered the three pre-existing error-path steps to `[26]`–`[28]`. Total: 28 numbered steps (was 19).

### Changed
- **ui.py NoneType race fix (v0.6.0 follow-up, surfaced during v0.6.1 smoke testing).** `PRAXOVA_PT_wall_framing.draw` previously read `context.active_object` twice in one draw call — once inside `_get_wall_source` and once directly into `active_obj`. When the two reads straddled Blender's mid-draw context update after deleting an active wall Empty, the resolver saw the wall (still valid) and returned a non-scratch source, but the next line read `active_obj.name` and got `AttributeError` on `None`. Fix: `_get_wall_source(context)` gains an optional `active` keyword — when the caller passes it, the resolver uses that value instead of re-reading. `draw` now reads `context.active_object` exactly once at the top of the method, caches it as `active_obj`, and threads the same value through every consumer (Fill source check, Wall Framing source resolver via `_get_wall_source(context, active=active_obj)`, the "Editing wall: …" gate, the Active Material readout). The six operator callers (`add_door`, `add_window`, `remove_opening`×2, `distribute_openings`×2) keep their existing `_get_wall_source(context)` signature — operator contexts don't hit the same mid-draw race because they run between draws.
- **`mcp_server` package version** 0.6.1 → 0.6.2. Project description extended to mention "L corner joints with multi-wall framing orchestration."
- **Smoke test `addon_version` pin** "0.6.1" → "0.6.2". Method count `14 → 18` (add_joint + remove_joint + frame_building + list_joints_in_level).
- **README** Status line "Beta (v0.6.1)" → "Beta (v0.6.2)". MCP Bridge tool list adds the four new methods. Roadmap marks v0.6.2 done.

### Architecture notes
- **Bridge joint operations write directly to the CollectionProperty.** `add_joint` calls `level.praxova_level.joints.add()` then assigns fields; `remove_joint` calls `.remove(joint_index)` after capturing the pre-removal wire spec. No `bpy.ops.praxova.add_joint` / `remove_joint` invocations. Mirrors v0.5.1's `frame_wall` opening pattern of populating `sp.wall_openings` directly rather than invoking `add_door` / `add_window` — the UI ops exist for human-driven "click + then fill" flows; a bridge caller has all fields up front so the one-step direct write fits better. The UI ops stay unchanged.
- **`list_joints_in_level` fills the gap v0.6.1's `list_objects` deliberately left.** The LEVEL category in v0.6.1 reported `joints_count` only because joint serialization needed the wire format that v0.6.2's `add_joint` / `remove_joint` finally standardize. The other three convenience list methods (`list_buildings`, `list_levels_in_building`, `list_walls_in_level`) stay deferred — v0.6.1's `list_objects` with category filtering already covers structural discovery without requiring the bridge to maintain duplicated query surfaces. Only joint detail was a genuine gap, because joints don't have Object identities.
- **`frame_building` return shape is per-wall summary, not full per-wall frame_wall echo.** Each `walls_framed` entry surfaces what's actually new about orchestration — `joint_indices_touching` (which joints reference this wall) and `n_corner_fillers` (interior fillers placed under this wall by the joint resolution) — plus enough geometry context (`n_studs`) to spot-check. Per-wall opening detail (`openings_summary`, header / jack lengths, cripple counts) is intentionally left for follow-up `frame_wall(wall_target=<wall_name>)` queries: the orchestrator's response stays focused on joint coordination, which is the actual point of orchestration over N separate `frame_wall` calls.
- **`validate_joints` shared between the Building tab live indicator and the bridge pre-flight.** Same first-failure surface with the `"Joint N: ..."` prefix — an LLM driving `frame_building` over the bridge sees the same error message a human sees in the Building tab's UIList. Matches v0.5.1's `frame_wall` + `validate_openings` integration pattern; consistent single-source-of-truth across the bridge.
- **Joint wire format wall references are Object name strings.** `wall_a_name` / `wall_b_name` brittleness — renaming a wall Empty in Blender invalidates any joint spec the LLM previously cached (the `PointerProperty` on the existing `PraxovaJoint` follows the rename via Blender's datablock pointer, but a fresh `add_joint` call with the old name hits `INVALID_PARAMS`). Same trade-off as `target_name` in `duplicate_oc` / `duplicate_e2e` — consistent across the bridge. Workaround: call `list_joints_in_level` to refresh canonical names before further joint mutation.
- **First time a UI cycle's bridge parity split across two minors.** v0.6 (UI) → v0.6.1 (containers + `frame_wall` extension + `list_objects` extension) → v0.6.2 (joints + `frame_building` + `list_joints_in_level`). Precedent for big UI cycles getting two-minor bridge follow-ups when shipping all parity in one cycle would create a hard-to-review handler diff. Method count progression: 12 → 14 → 18.
- **The 6 v0.6.2 commits.** (0) `ui.py`: cache `context.active_object` once + `_get_wall_source` signature change — fixes a v0.6.0 NoneType race surfaced during v0.6.1 smoke testing, before the cycle proper begins. (1) `server/handlers` + `mcp`: `add_joint` + `remove_joint` bridge parity, plus the four shared helpers (`_resolve_level`, `_resolve_wall_in_level`, `_parse_joint`, `_joint_to_wire`) the remaining commits reuse. (2) `server/handlers` + `mcp`: `frame_building` orchestrator. (3) `server/handlers` + `mcp`: `list_joints_in_level` query. (4) `smoke_test`: 9-step extension covering the full joint flow. (5) This release commit — manifest + `__version__` + smoke_test pin + mcp pkg version + CHANGELOG + README.

## [0.6.1] — 2026-05-12

### Added
- **Bridge `add_building` handler + FastMCP tool.** Creates a Building Empty plus a default Level child in one call, matching the UI's `praxova.add_building` operator. Optional `name` (auto-generated `Building_NN` if omitted, with upfront collision check against `bpy.data.objects`), `location_m` (world-space meters; default origin), and `default_corner_style` (only `"THREE_STUD"` valid in v0.6 — exposed for forward-compat with v0.7's California / ladder-blocking styles). Returns `building_name`, `level_name`, `level_index` (always 0 for the default Level), `location_m`, `default_corner_style`.
- **Bridge `add_level` handler + FastMCP tool.** Adds another Level under an existing Praxova-managed Building. Required `building` (Empty name; resolves to `APP_OBJECT_NOT_FOUND` (-32004) if missing or not a Praxova Building). Optional `name` (auto-generated, collision-checked) and `level_height_in` (default sourced from `PraxovaLevelProps.level_height_in`'s PropertyGroup default — never hardcoded in the handler). Returns `level_name`, `level_index` (auto-assigned to max+1 of existing Level indices under this Building), `building`, `level_height_in`.
- **Bridge `frame_wall` extended with `level`, `wall_target`, `rotation_deg_z`.** Two explicit modes picked by whether `wall_target` is set:
  - **CREATE** (default): build a new wall Empty + children. `length_in` and `height_in` still REQUIRED. `level` (new) parents the wall under the specified Level Empty so it can be joined with siblings; omitted = top-level wall (exact v0.5.1 behavior).
  - **REBUILD** (`wall_target` set): re-frame an existing wall in place. The Empty itself is preserved (name, joint references, transform unless overridden); only children get deleted + rebuilt. **WRITE-THROUGH** semantics: each scalar param provided writes onto the wall's `PraxovaWallProps`; each omitted preserves the existing value. `length_in` and `height_in` become OPTIONAL. `openings` follows the same rule — omit to preserve, provide a list (including `[]`) to fully replace.
  - `rotation_deg_z` (new in both modes): Z-axis rotation in degrees applied to the wall Empty. CREATE: 0 if omitted. REBUILD: preserves current rotation if omitted. **JOINTS LOCK ROTATION**: in REBUILD mode, if `rotation_deg_z` differs (via `math.isclose` with `rel_tol=1e-6, abs_tol=1e-9` on radians) from the wall's current rotation AND its parent Level has any joint referencing this wall, the call is REFUSED with `INVALID_PARAMS` and a message telling the user to remove the joint via the Joints UIList in Blender first. Refusal happens before write-through so a rejected call leaves the wall untouched.
  - Return shape gains three always-present fields alongside the v0.5.1 set: `wall_target_used` (bool — True for REBUILD), `level_name` (str | None — the wall's parent Level Empty name), `rotation_deg_z` (final Z rotation in degrees).
  - Validation order: argument resolution (level / wall_target / rotation_deg_z / location), then mode-specific scalar reads with their respective defaults, then shared cross-field validation, then openings parse + write-through, then op invocation.
- **Bridge `list_objects` extended for the v0.6 hierarchy Empties.** `praxova_only` filter now uses a new `_is_praxova_managed(obj)` helper that checks all four role discriminators (`is_lumber`, `is_wall`, `is_level`, `is_building`). Wall / Level / Building Empties were previously silently skipped — they now appear in the listing. `_serialize_object` refactored to dispatch on role in priority order (wall, level, building, then v0.4 material) with dedicated helpers:
  - **WALL**: `name`, `category="WALL"`, `location_m`, `rotation_deg_z` (degrees), `parent_level` (name or None), `length_in`, `height_in`, `nominal`, `spacing_oc_in`, `top_plate_count`, `bottom_plate_count`, `openings` (list of dicts in v0.5.1 wire format — so a wall returned here round-trips cleanly into `frame_wall(wall_target=..., openings=<echoed list>)`).
  - **LEVEL**: `name`, `category="LEVEL"`, `level_index`, `level_height_in`, `parent_building` (name or None), `contained_wall_names` (sorted alphabetically), `wall_count`, `joints_count` (count only — full joint serialization is v0.6.2).
  - **BUILDING**: `name`, `category="BUILDING"`, `location_m`, `default_corner_style`, `contained_level_names` (sorted by `level_index`), `level_count`.
- **Smoke test steps `[11]` – `[14]` exercising the v0.6.1 surface.** `[11]` `add_building` with explicit `name="TestBuilding"` (asserts the rename took, default Level created, `level_index == 0`). `[12]` `add_level` under TestBuilding (asserts `level_index == 1`, echo of `building`). `[13]` CREATE frame_wall with `level=<from [12]>`, `rotation_deg_z=90`, `length_in=48`, `height_in=96` (asserts `wall_name`, `n_studs == 4`, `n_plates == 3`, `level_name` matches, `rotation_deg_z ≈ 90`, `wall_target_used is False`). `[14]` REBUILD frame_wall with `wall_target=<wall from [13]>`, `length_in=72` (asserts Empty name preserved, `length_in == 72`, `n_studs == 6`, `wall_target_used is True`, `level_name` preserved, `rotation_deg_z ≈ 90` preserved). Renumbered `[15]` `list_objects` count assertion `32 -> 47` (32 baseline lumber + 2 v0.6 Wall Empties now visible + 13 new from the four v0.6.1 steps).

### Architecture notes
- **v0.6 bridge parity split into v0.6.1 + v0.6.2.** v0.6 was the largest UI cycle to date (per-wall PropertyGroup refactor, three new container classes, joint helpers, multi-tab UI, ten total commits). Splitting bridge parity by feature scope keeps each cycle focused and reviewable. v0.6.1 ships the container creators (`add_building`, `add_level`) and the structural `frame_wall` extension (CREATE under Level, REBUILD in place, rotation) plus the `list_objects` enumeration of the v0.6 Empties. v0.6.2 will ship the joint operators (`add_joint`, `remove_joint`), the `frame_building` orchestrator, and the dedicated `list_buildings` / `list_levels_in_building` / `list_walls_in_level` / `list_joints_in_level` methods. Method count grows 12 → 14 → ~19, all incremental.
- **Write-through semantics for `wall_target`.** REBUILD mode mirrors how the UI's "active wall + Frame Wall click" workflow operates: changes you make in the panel get persisted to `PraxovaWallProps`, then the operator rebuilds children from those props. The bridge accepts the same params and writes them through the same way — partial updates work cleanly (change just `length_in`, preserve everything else) and the wall's own props remain the authoritative storage. Openings get the same omit-vs-provide treatment: omit to preserve the existing collection, provide a list (even `[]`) to fully replace.
- **Rotation+joints refusal.** A rotated wall under a Level with joints would invalidate the joints' implicit world-space alignment assumption (the joint connects two walls at a specific world-space corner). v0.6.1 refuses the rotation explicitly rather than silently breaking the joint, with a message that points the user at the in-Blender UI to clean up. The refusal happens before write-through so the wall stays in a consistent state on rejection. Rotation epsilon is `math.isclose(..., rel_tol=1e-6, abs_tol=1e-9)` on the radian values — no angle wraparound; passing 360 means literal 360°.
- **`list_objects` shape preserves v0.4 → v0.6 backward compatibility.** The existing LUMBER / SHEET / PANEL branches are unchanged byte-for-byte. ### Architecture notes
- **In-place re-framing preserves PointerProperty references.** Re-framing a wall via the Wall Framing tab deletes the wall Empty's children and rebuilds them from the wall's own `PraxovaWallProps`, but the Empty itself stays. Joint references to that wall Empty (from `PraxovaJoint.wall_a` / `wall_b` PointerProperty fields on the Level's joints collection) survive the re-frame intact. Same goes for the wall's name, transform, and Level parent. Without this preservation, every wall edit would invalidate joints and force the user to re-pick walls in every joint — the multi-wall workflow would be unusable.
- **Joint geometry lives in pure helpers; the operator does the bpy plumbing.** `templates.compute_wall_joint_context`, `l_corner_geometry`, `validate_joints` have no `bpy.*` calls — they're reasonable about and unit-testable without Blender in the loop, same as the v0.4 composite and v0.5 opening helpers. The operator (`_resolve_joint_context` + the modified plate / stud / filler placement loops) does the scene mutation. `reframe_wall_in_place` from the design doc collapsed into `frame_wall`'s in-place mode rather than living as a separate helper, because the bpy plumbing is identical for the user-driven case and the orchestrator-driven case.
- **`frame_building` as a coordination-only orchestrator.** Iterates `level.children`, sets each wall as the active object, invokes `bpy.ops.praxova.frame_wall('EXEC_DEFAULT')`. No new geometry math — single source of truth is `frame_wall`'s in-place mode + `_resolve_joint_context`. Joint validation runs once up front, not per wall, so a bad joint surfaces as one clean error instead of N per-wall failures.
- **Per-plate mesh dispatch.** v0.5 created a single shared `plate_mesh` per wall (all plates at full wall length). v0.6 dropped the shared mesh because joints can shorten individual plates by the paired wall's stud thickness. Each unique plate length gets its own `praxova_mesh_wall_plate_<nominal>_<length>` mesh, looked up via `_get_or_make_mesh` inside the placement loop. Two walls with identical plate lengths share mesh data naturally; orphan meshes from re-framing are left for Blender's purge to clean up on file save.
- **End stud overrides applied before the opening filter.** ABUTTING walls move their end stud inboard by the paired wall's stud thickness via `mods[end]['end_stud_override']`. The override applies to `raw_stud_xs_in` before the opening filter, so the v0.5 `filter_studs_around_openings` already sees the corner-aware positions — no second pass needed and no risk that a king-bracket overlap with a moved end stud goes uncaught.
- **Three-stud corner naming and ownership.** The interior filler stud lives under the MAIN wall's Empty (never ABUTTING — per the design doc table; only one filler per joint, on the main wall's side). Object name follows the design's `CornerFiller_Joint<NN>` convention; the joint index is resolved by scanning `level.praxova_level.joints` for the joint that touches this wall at this end.

  ```
  LUMBER — CUT LIST
        3  x  2x4x8 H
        8  x  2x4x8 V  → cut to 91.5"

  LUMBER — ORDER SUMMARY
       11  x  2x4x8  (3 H, 8 V)
  ```

### Architecture notes
- **Composite operator separation of concerns.** `ops_frame.py` is a new module distinct from `ops_add.py` (single-piece placement) and `ops_duplicate.py` / `ops_fill.py` (single-axis arrays). The naming convention is `ops_<verb>.py` — add, duplicate, fill, frame, cutlist, units. Future cycles will add door/window openings and corner stud treatments in the same module.
- **Pure-function helpers in `templates.py`.** The three new helpers have no `bpy.` references, so the math is reasonable about without Blender in the loop. They live in a new "Composite framing helpers (v0.4)" section between mesh construction and the v0.1 backward-compat aliases. Future composite operators will add their helpers in the same section. Promotion to a separate `helpers.py` module is deferred until that section earns its own file.
- **Empty parent at world origin, children placed in world coordinates.** The wall Empty lands at `(0, 0, 0)` and each plate/stud is created with its world-coordinate `location` set explicitly. Then `child.parent = empty` happens after placement. Because the parent is at origin, the child's local location equals its world location — no `matrix_parent_inverse` fiddling required. The user transforms the Empty afterward to position the wall in the larger model; children follow.

No collisions even when dimensions coincide. Two `frame_wall` calls with identical parameters share meshes naturally.

### Architecture notes
- The bridge runs a background listener thread inside Blender; each client connection spawns its own thread, but all bpy mutation is dispatched through `bpy.app.timers.register` onto Blender's main thread so concurrent connections cannot race on bpy state.
